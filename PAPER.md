# ProbeID, replicated on scripted tools: behaviour beats descriptions, exact-match ties on deterministic tools, tolerance wins under noise

**Open replication of arXiv 2610.33633** (Chen, Zhang, He, Lv, Huang,
Huang, "ProbeID: Verifying MCP Tool Identity for LLM Agents under
Registry Drift", submitted 24 Oct 2026). Independent clean-room
implementation; no author code exists as of 2026-10-29 (the paper states
its code "will be released upon acceptance").

## 1. Claim

An LLM agent selects tools by their advertised descriptions. A squatter
can copy a trusted tool's description while implementing different
behaviour, and no description matcher can recover the intended tool from
identical descriptions. ProbeID instead identifies a tool by its
behaviour: at admission, canonicalized responses to schema-valid probes
are recorded as a trusted reference; at selection, candidates are probed
on a secret subsample and the nearest fingerprint wins, with abstention
when no candidate matches (distance gate) or the decision is ambiguous
(gap gate). This replication asks three questions on scripted tools:

1. **Disease:** do description routers collapse under copied descriptions
   while behavioural probing still identifies the gold?
2. **Concession:** does exact-match comparison tie ProbeID on
   deterministic tools, as the paper's own abstract concedes?
3. **Load-bearing:** is there a regime where ProbeID-style probing beats
   the strongest trivial baseline — and if so, what exactly does the
   work?

## 2. Method

**Tools.** 120 synthetic gold tools (12 families × 10 parameterizations:
addition, multiplication, powers, modulo, clamping, string transforms,
currency/unit conversion, list aggregates, date arithmetic, character
counting), each with a typed schema and a plain-sentence description.
Every response is a 3-leaf mapping `{result, unit, note}`, so the bounded
distance has a graded scale.

**Probes.** A 64-probe bank per gold, generated by type-aware enumeration
over boundary values. The headline uses the first m=12 probes (fixed
cache-order, as in the paper's headline); noise and gate studies draw m
probes at random without replacement.

**Squatters** copy the gold description verbatim and mutate behaviour:
full drift, 50% / 30% drift (per probe instance), silent field drop,
input-conditioned drift, near-twin (differs on exactly 1 bank probe),
twin (identical on all bank probes, differs only outside bank support),
and — in the noise regime — a reimplemented squatter with independent
benign jitter plus 30% drift.

**Verifiers.** ProbeID strict choice (nearest fingerprint; a tie for
first scores as a miss, per the paper's adversarial tie-breaking) and
Algorithm 1 gated choice (τ_hi, γ_min = 2ε, δ = 0.05, theory-set
thresholds). Baselines: exact-match distance, a BM25-style lexical
router, and a TF-IDF cosine router. Copied descriptions make all
candidates tie on router score; ties break by seeded random draw, so a
router's expected hit@1 is chance 1/(k+1).

**Noise regime.** The gold is stochastic: its numeric result jitters by
±0.02 on each call (positive self-distance, as in the paper's census
where 16/59 real tools show noise). Bounded distance uses a 0.05 numeric
tolerance, so jitter is invisible to it but a full mismatch for exact
equality. The squatter reimplements the tool with independent jitter
from the same distribution plus 30% drift.

**Statistics.** Paired bootstrap 95% CIs (10,000 resamples, gold-clustered,
paired by gold). Bars were preregistered (PREREG.md) after a pilot on
seed 0 and frozen before the head-to-head on seed 101. The head-to-head
was re-run with seed 101 and reproduced byte-identical results
(SHA-256 `0b2cfc5c00bd659a402441a408136a0fdb68d42d10754115cb0f722dd3f47358`).

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Disease: routers collapse, probing identifies (Gate 2, PASS)

Deterministic regime, k=8 squatters, m=12 probes, n=120 golds, strict
choice:

| Method | hit@1 |
|---|---|
| ProbeID (bounded distance) | **0.983** (118/120) |
| Exact-match | 0.983 (118/120) |
| BM25 router | 0.083 (10/120) |
| TF-IDF router | 0.125 (15/120) |

Gap ProbeID − BM25: **0.900, 95% CI [0.833, 0.950]**. Description routers
sit at chance (1/9 = 0.111) because squatters copy the description;
behavioural probing identifies the gold in 118 of 120 cases. The 2
misses are the paper's own miss mechanism: small-φ drift with no drifted
probe among the 12 sampled (probability 0.7^12 ≈ 1.4% per squatter).

### 3.2 The concession, reproduced exactly

On deterministic tools, ProbeID and exact-match agree on **all 120
cases** (gap 0.000, CI [0.000, 0.000]). This is structural in this
harness — bounded distance is 0 iff all leaves agree iff canonicalized
responses are equal iff exact distance is 0, and the gold sits at 0
under both rules — and it matches the paper's abstract: "simpler exact
comparisons tie it". Anyone claiming probing beats exact matching on
deterministic tools is wrong, here and in the paper.

### 3.3 Load-bearing: tolerance wins under noise (Gate 3, PASS)

Noise regime, k=8 squatters, m=32 probes, n=50 numeric golds:

| Method | hit@1 |
|---|---|
| ProbeID (bounded, tol 0.05) | **1.000** (50/50) |
| Exact-match | 0.360 (18/50) |

Gap: **0.640, 95% CI [0.500, 0.780]**. Mechanism: the gold's self-distance
under exact equality is ≈0.67 (independent jitter draws disagree on
two-thirds of probes), while the squatter's is ≈0.77 — a gap of only
0.08 with sampling noise ≈0.08 at m=32, so exact ranking is
noise-dominated. Bounded distance with tolerance sees the gold at ≈0
and the squatter at ≈0.10 (30% drift × one leaf of three), a clean
separation. The honest statement of ProbeID's advantage is therefore
not "probing beats exact matching" but "tolerance-aware comparison beats
exact comparison when benign noise exceeds the adversarial drift margin".

### 3.4 Gate study: refusal budgets and the uncatchable cases

Gold-absent single-candidate gate, theory-set thresholds, 40 golds per
cell:

| Replacement | m=8 | m=32 | m=64 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reachable, equality — refused | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| Reachable, bounded — refused | 0.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| Small-margin (near-twin), equality — refused | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.125 |
| Small-margin, bounded — refused | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.000 |
| Twin, either — refused | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.000 |
| Gold alone, either — refused | 0.000 (m=32) | | |

The direction matches the paper's Table 1 (equality refuses reachable
replacements with fewer probes than bounded distance); the absolute
budgets differ (paper: 64 vs 512) because margins differ across
regimes — reported as a regime difference, not a reproduction. The two
limitations the paper states are both reproduced: small-margin drift
passes because the worst-case radius cannot resolve so small a margin,
and twins whose drift no bank probe reaches **always pass** (0.000
refusal at every budget, under both comparisons).

Tainted-task analog (16 scripted tasks, single-candidate equality gate,
m=32): 6 of 14 squatter tasks accepted (4 small-margin + 2 twin), 8 of 8
reachable refused, 2 of 2 gold controls accepted. This is an ANALOG of
the paper's 15 → 9 of 16, not a reproduction (different gate setup);
it shows the same structure — verification removes the reachable
rug pulls and leaves exactly the coverage-limited ones.

## 4. Limits

- **Synthetic everything.** Tools, descriptions, squatters and probes are
  scripted. The paper's benchmark figures (hit@1 0.956 on cached
  MCP-IdentityDrift cases, tainted tasks 15 → 9 of 16, refusal from
  64/512 probes) are context only and are NOT claimed as reproduced.
- **Tolerance is load-bearing.** The noise-regime advantage assumes
  benign jitter inside a fixed 0.05 tolerance. Real tool noise may
  exceed any fixed tolerance, shrinking the advantage; conversely, a
  squatter whose drift is smaller than the tolerance also passes.
- **Coverage is the binding constraint.** Anything no sampled probe
  reaches is uncatchable by construction — twins, small margins, and
  side effects that never reach a response (the paper's exfiltration
  caveat). A verification verdict is only as good as its probe bank.
- **Reference integrity assumed.** If the admission reference was
  recorded from a compromised tool, every later verdict inherits the
  compromise. The paper states this; this replication does not test it.
- **No LLM agents.** Routers are lexical; a real LLM reranker might
  behave differently — but it cannot beat chance on identical
  descriptions either, since the identifying information is absent.

## 5. Conclusion

On scripted tools, the ProbeID mechanism does what the paper claims
where it matters: it restores identification when descriptions are
copied (0.983 vs 0.083, CI excluding 0), it ties exact matching on
deterministic tools exactly as the paper concedes, and its real
advantage over the trivial baseline appears under value noise, where
tolerance-aware comparison separates a noisy gold from a small-drift
squatter that exact equality cannot (0.640 gap, CI [0.500, 0.780]).
And it fails exactly where the paper says it fails: drift no probe
reaches always passes. Verification is a probe-coverage claim, not a
proof of identity.
